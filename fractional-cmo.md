A **fractional CMO** is a senior marketing executive who works part-time for your company, usually 10 to 20 hours a week on a monthly retainer, and owns marketing the way a full-time chief marketing officer would: strategy, budget, team, vendors and results. Companies hire one when they need executive marketing leadership but don't need, or can't yet justify, a full-time CMO.

That's the definition. Here's the part most explainers leave out: a fractional CMO is only as good as the playbook they bring. Many are still running the inbound, MQL-driven model that stopped working when B2B buyers moved their research into AI assistants, peer communities and private channels. Hiring one of those part-time just gets you the old playbook at a discount.

This guide covers what a fractional CMO actually does, how the role compares to an agency, a consultant or a full-time hire, what it costs, and when it's the right call. It's written by [Tangyslice Collective](/about/), a two-person fractional CMO and AI-native marketing consultancy in Massachusetts.

## What does a fractional CMO do?

A fractional CMO sets marketing strategy, owns the marketing budget and plan, leads the people and vendors who execute it, and reports results to the CEO and board. The difference from an advisor is accountability: a fractional CMO is on the hook for pipeline, not for the quality of a slide deck.

Core fractional CMO responsibilities:

1. **Strategy and positioning.** Define who you sell to, why they buy, and what you say that competitors can't. Everything else depends on this.
2. **Plan and budget.** Turn strategy into a quarterly plan with a budget, channel mix and targets the CFO will sign off on.
3. **Team leadership.** Manage, coach and hire the marketing team. Decide what to build in-house and what to contract.
4. **Vendor and agency management.** Hold agencies, freelancers and software vendors to outcomes. Cut the ones that only report activity.
5. **Measurement and reporting.** Pick the handful of metrics that predict revenue and report them honestly to leadership and the board.
6. **Sales alignment.** Agree with sales on what counts as a real opportunity, so marketing and sales stop arguing about lead quality.
7. **Go-to-market for launches.** Own the market side of product launches, pricing changes and entry into new segments.

## Fractional CMO vs. full-time CMO vs. agency vs. consultant

### What is a fractional CMO agency?

A fractional CMO agency, or firm, is a company that supplies fractional CMOs rather than a single independent executive. Some are marketplaces that match you with a vetted freelancer. Others, like Tangyslice, are small partnerships where the founders do the work themselves. A firm can bring a bench of skills that one person can't, but ask who actually shows up each week: the person who sold you or someone junior.

## How much does a fractional CMO cost?

A fractional CMO typically costs **$3,000 to $20,000 a month** on retainer, or **$150 to $500 an hour**, depending on hours, seniority and scope. Industry pricing guides put full-time CMO compensation at roughly $175,000 to $300,000 or more before benefits, equity and recruiting fees, so a fractional CMO usually costs a fraction of a full-time hire.

The retainer is rarely the whole cost. Budget separately for the programs the CMO runs: paid media, content, tools and any agencies they keep. A good fractional CMO should cut some of that spend in the first quarter.

## When should you hire a fractional CMO?

Hire a fractional CMO when marketing needs an owner and a full-time executive would be too much, too soon, or too slow to find. The common signs:

- You don't have a head of marketing, and the CEO or a founder is still making every marketing call.
- You have marketers executing without a clear strategy, and activity is up while pipeline isn't.
- Pipeline slowed and nobody can explain what changed, even though spend stayed the same.
- You're paying agencies and tools you can't evaluate, because nobody senior owns them.
- You're preparing for a launch, a new market or a fundraise and need a credible marketing plan fast.
- You plan to hire a full-time CMO in a year or two and want the foundation, team and metrics in place first.

When it's the wrong call: if you're pre-product-market fit, you need founder-led selling and a scrappy generalist more than an executive. And if you have a large in-house team and complex enterprise sales cycles, you probably need a full-time CMO.

## What changes when buyers research with AI?

Your buyers now ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude and Google's AI Overviews which vendors to consider, long before they visit your site or fill in a form. If you're not in that answer, you're not on the shortlist, and none of it shows up in your analytics.

That changes the fractional CMO job in three ways:

1. **Visibility in AI answers becomes a pipeline metric.** A modern fractional CMO checks what AI assistants say about your company and your category, and fixes it with structured content, entity building and third-party mentions. We call this [AI visibility and generative search optimization](/services/#visibility).
2. **The MQL stops being the scoreboard.** Buyers who arrive already decided don't download three ebooks first. We explain why in [The MQL Was Always a Fiction](/blog/why-mqls-dont-work/).
3. **AI workflows replace headcount you can't afford.** Research, content production and lead intelligence that used to need an agency can run as [AI workflows you own](/services/#systems), in your stack, on your data.

**A fractional CMO in 2026 has to win the AI answer, not just the Google ranking.**
